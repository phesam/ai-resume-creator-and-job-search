# Work and accomplishments inventory

This file is the only source of truth about you. The resume, the job reviews and every application answer are built
from it, and nothing goes on the resume that is not in it. The inventory interview fills it in by asking you
questions; you can also write in it directly.

Rules for this file (they came out of a week of resume work; docs/RESUME_PLAYBOOK.md explains each):
- **Facts, not resume text.** An answer recorded here is raw material, not a request to put it on the resume.
- **Your part, not the team's.** Write what you personally did, and what you did not do ("built the GraphQL layer
  in front of the CMS; did not build the CMS").
- **Every number says what it measures**, from what to what, over what period, and where it comes from. A number
  without that gets asked about, never silently dropped.
- **Status is exact:** shipped or not, A/B tested then rolled out, merged or in review, achieved or projected.
- **Date each addition** (`### Added 2026-10-05`). When two entries disagree, the newer one wins and the old one
  gets a note.

## <Employer> — <Title> (<Mon YYYY> – <Mon YYYY or Present>)

Copy this block for every job, internship, startup, open-source contribution and serious project.

**In one sentence a stranger understands:** what the product or system is and who uses it.

**Scale:** users, traffic (views/week, requests/day), data volume, money, number of teams. Say which numbers you
measured and which you were told.

**Why it existed / the problem before:** what was broken, slow, missing or manual, and who was hurt by it. Who couldn't do
what before this, and at what level of the organization? Why did it matter to the company, in one sentence?

**What you personally did:** your part, in your words. Who else worked on it and what they did. What you owned
end to end. Decisions you made.

**Stack you actually used:** only things you could be questioned on in an interview.

**The hardest part, and what it took:** what made it difficult (unknown internal systems, failing deploys, a race condition,
a tight deadline, conflicting teams). What would have happened if it stayed broken? How you found and fixed it.

**Results (from → to):** each with what it measures, the baseline, the period and the source.

**Adoption and recognition:** who uses it now beyond your own team, teams onboarded, awards, who you presented to.

**What past interviewers asked about or praised:** this gets protected from later cuts.

**People you worked with:** senior partners by role (head of Product, design lead), and how many teams you got aligned.

**Status:** shipped / A/B tested then rolled out / in review / merged / projected.

**How you'd say it out loud:** two or three sentences in your own words.

**Menu (big projects only):** everything you built on it with your share, including shipping and release work.

**Do not claim:** overstatements to avoid (for example "did not architect the platform", "never used AWS").

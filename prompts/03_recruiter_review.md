# Prompt 3: an independent review

Use a NEW chat, so the reviewer isn't anchored on the conversation that wrote the bullets. Paste the resume text (copy
it out of the PDF, so you're reviewing what software actually extracts), your inventory for the claims check, and your
decisions log so it doesn't re-propose what you settled.

---

Review my resume the way it will really be read. Don't rewrite it wholesale; give findings and specific proposed
wordings that use only facts in my inventory.

1. **Is the page finished?** A finished page holds 6–8 entries and 13–15 bullets of about 195 characters, with about three
   technical terms per bullet, and fills the page. If mine is thin or plain, say so first: that needs more material, not
   rewording.
2. **Software's read.** Is the text in the right order? Name first? Contact details intact? Any odd characters
   (non-breaking hyphens, special spaces, icons)? Standard section headings? Do the technologies I really used appear
   inside bullets, not only in the skills lines?
3. **Recruiter's 30-second skim** (assume no engineering background and a keyword list). After the skim, from memory:
   which employers and titles stand out; for each job, what I built and why it mattered; the single most impressive thing
   and whether it's near the top; any private names. Public technical terms are for the engineer: never suggest removing one.
4. **Senior engineer's read**, bullet by bullet. This is the read that decides. Real work, or a description of a task? Impressed,
   or just informed? Does it say what, how and why? Is the scope honest in both directions, neither overclaimed nor undersold? Does
   each number say what it measures? Is my hardest work on the page, with the failure named? Is the best result next to its
   baseline? Any filler, stiff verbs, identical bullet shapes, or process words where a concrete noun belongs?
5. **Claims check:** for every bullet, point to the inventory fact that supports it. List anything missing, stronger
   than the inventory says, or with an outdated status.

Report: a two-sentence verdict, then findings in priority order (what's wrong, which reader it hurts, why, and a
proposed rewording), then what's already strong and should be left alone. The page is full, so every addition must name what it
replaces. Don't re-propose anything my decisions log settled. Say how strong the evidence behind each recommendation is; don't
present opinion as fact.

# Prompt 3: an independent review

Use a NEW chat, so the reviewer isn't anchored on the conversation that wrote the bullets. Paste the resume text (copy
it out of the PDF, so you're reviewing what software actually extracts) and, for the claims check, your inventory.

---

Review my resume the way it will really be read. Don't rewrite it wholesale; give findings and specific proposed
wordings that use only facts in my inventory.

1. **Software's read.** Is the text in the right order? Name first? Contact details intact? Any odd characters
   (non-breaking hyphens, special spaces, icons)? Standard section headings? Do the technologies I really used appear
   inside bullets, not only in the skills lines?
2. **Recruiter's 30-second skim** (assume no engineering background and a keyword list). After the skim, from memory:
   which employers and titles stand out; for each job, what I built and why it mattered in plain words; the single
   most impressive thing and whether it's near the top; anything you couldn't understand.
3. **Hiring manager's read**, bullet by bullet: does it say what, how and why? Is the scope honest? Does each number
   say what it measures? Is there at least one hard engineering problem solved? Is the best result next to its
   baseline? Any filler, stiff verbs, identical bullet shapes, walls of text, or more than one bold per bullet?
4. **Claims check:** for every bullet, point to the inventory fact that supports it. List anything missing, stronger
   than the inventory says, or with an outdated status.

Report: a two-sentence verdict, then findings in priority order (what's wrong, which reader it hurts, why, and a
proposed rewording), then what's already strong and should be left alone. Say how strong the evidence behind each
recommendation is; don't present opinion as fact.

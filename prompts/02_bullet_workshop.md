# Prompt 2: the bullet workshop

Paste everything below the line into a new chat, with your inventory and, if you have one, a few postings you'd
apply to. Work through one job at a time.

---

You are helping me write my one-page software engineering resume, one bullet at a time, from my inventory below. The
inventory is the only source of facts; never add or inflate anything. If a fact you need is missing, stop and ask me
(all missing questions in one numbered list).

Who reads it: (1) screening software that extracts the text and matches skills by meaning; (2) a recruiter who is
not an engineer, skims for 7–30 seconds, and may hold a keyword list; (3) a hiring manager who reads the bullets that
survive and interviews me on them. Every bullet must work for all three.

Every bullet = WHAT I built or changed (a noun a stranger recognizes) + HOW (the real technologies, inside the
sentence) + WHY it mattered (who it was for, what changed, how big). Example:
"Built the Node.js/GraphQL service that lets clinic staff change their schedules (~2M requests/day), so a schedule
change takes ~2 minutes instead of a support ticket and a next-day release"

Rules:
- A list of tools or setup tasks with no purpose fails, even if every keyword is one postings ask for.
- Name the system and its scale. Put the best result where a skimmer sees it, with its contrast right next to it.
- My real scope only ("added X through a service", never "built the platform" if I built one layer).
- Don't diminish my best work ("prototype", "built the backend").
- About two lines: one for what, one for why. Plain past-tense verbs (built, rebuilt, fixed, added, cut). No
  "spearheaded", "leveraged", "utilized", "various", "increasing developer velocity", no first person.
- Bold at most one thing per bullet.
- No internal names or acronyms an outsider can't read; no team size, process trivia or small counts that don't
  impress.
- Never drop one of my numbers without asking me what it measures. Exact status: projected vs achieved, merged vs in
  review.
- Use a posting's word only for work I actually did.
- Strongest bullet first in each job.
- Use plain ASCII hyphens and spaces only (no non-breaking hyphens or narrow no-break spaces).

Process: for the job I name, propose 3–5 wordings of ONE bullet at a time and wait for my pick or edits before the
next. Don't rewrite bullets I've approved. When a bullet is too long, keep it as one bullet and drop its least
important part. Keep a running list of decisions and rejected wording at the end of each reply, one line each.

Layout I'll use: one page, one column; header with name, phone, email, LinkedIn, GitHub (and "U.S. Citizen" if I
target export-controlled work), no city, no summary; Experience, then Projects, then Education (degree and GPA on one
line), then two Skills lines of things I can be questioned on.

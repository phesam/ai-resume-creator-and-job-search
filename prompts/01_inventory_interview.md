# Prompt 1: the inventory interview

Paste everything below the line into a new chat. Paste your old resume (or LinkedIn "Experience" text) where it says

---

You are going to interview me about my work so we can build an inventory: a complete, factual record of everything
I've done that might ever go on a resume. You are not writing my resume yet.

Rules:
- Ask me everything you need in ONE numbered list, grouped by job or project. There is no limit on the number of
  questions. I'll answer in any order, maybe in fragments.
- For every job, internship, startup, open-source contribution and serious project, find out:
  1. what it is, in one sentence a stranger would understand, and who uses it;
  2. why it existed: what was broken, slow, missing or manual before, and who that hurt;
  3. what I personally did, what others did, what I owned end to end, decisions I made;
  4. what was hard (unknown systems, failures, bugs, deadlines, disagreements) and how I solved it;
  5. scale (users, traffic, requests, data, money, teams);
  6. results from what to what, and for each number: what it measures, the baseline, the period, the source;
  7. exact status (shipped, A/B tested, merged, projected);
  8. adoption and recognition; who I worked with by role;
  9. the stack I actually used, and what I did not use;
  10. the plain meaning of every internal name or acronym;
  11. how I'd describe it out loud to a friend outside tech.
- Never invent or assume a fact. If I don't know something, record that I don't.
- An answer is raw material, not an instruction to put it on the resume.
- When I've answered, give me the inventory as a markdown document: one section per entry with the fields above,
  then a "Skills" section (used for real / coursework only / don't list), then a dated "Interview log" with my exact
  answers. Then list anything still missing as a new numbered list of questions.
